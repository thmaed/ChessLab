Privacy

Nothing leaves your device.

Most chess apps want an account, a connection and your data. ChessLab wants none of them — and you can check.

  • Works in airplane mode

    The engine, the human-like opponents, the analysis, the courses and the puzzles are all built in and compute on your device.

  • No account, no tracking

    No sign-up, no analytics, no ads, no third-party service. We have no server, so there is nothing to collect your data.

  • Verifiable, open source

    The Android app does not even request the INTERNET permission, so it cannot connect to anything. The full source code is public (GPLv3).

For every kind of player

ChessLab runs several engines, including ones that play like humans, so the same app suits a first game and a club tournament.

  • Just starting out

    Play against a gentle character who makes human mistakes, ask for a hint when you are stuck, and let the Coach explain what went wrong — in one plain sentence.

  • Improving at the club

    Build your repertoire with explained openings, drill it with spaced repetition, and turn your own mistakes into puzzles.

  • Curious and tinkering

    Twelve variants, Chess960, a laboratory that pits two Stockfish settings against each other, and PGN, FEN and photo import.

Features

Everything you need to get better

Play, understand, remember. Each part works on its own, and together they make a daily habit.

01

Opponents that play like humans

Nine characters played by Maia-3, a network trained on millions of human games: it does not look for the best move, it plays the one a human of that level would play, blunders included. Each has a style, a repertoire and a temperament, and a level set on the human scale. Or take on Stockfish, from beginner (~900 Elo) to maximum (~3190). Optional clock, hints, a warning before risky moves and an evaluation bar, all on demand.

02

Analyse and understand

Every move is graded (inaccuracy, mistake, blunder, brilliant…), each player gets an accuracy score, and the app tells you why a move was bad. The Coach adds the share of players at your level who play it. At the end, it offers to turn your mistakes and missed mates into puzzles. Load a game by PGN, FEN, position editor or photo scanner.

03

Daily session

Every day, what to review: opening positions that are due, missed puzzles, an endgame. A streak of days and medals to keep you going.

04

Openings

58 hand-written openings (multilingual), checked against the engine: every move is explained, coloured arrows tie the board to the move list, and spaced repetition makes them stick. Import your own repertoires as PGN too.

05

Endgames

78 courses proven by endgame tablebases: no move taught gives up a win, no defence offered loses a draw. Plus free training, where any move that keeps the verdict is accepted.

06

Puzzles

Over 130,000 problems from the Lichess database, filtered by level and theme, plus your own: drawn from your mistakes, or imported from your own PGN files. Personal rating and “At my level”.

07

Variants and more

Chess960 and eleven variants against the computer (King of the Hill, Three-Check, Horde, Atomic, Crazyhouse, Duck Chess, Barricades…), each with its own analysis. Two-player mode, and a Laboratory to compare two Stockfish settings.

FAQ

Questions, answered

Is ChessLab really free?

Yes. It is free, with no ads and no account. It is a one-person project, and the source code is open.

Does it need an internet connection?

No. Everything works offline, in airplane mode: the engines, the analysis, the courses and the puzzles are all inside the app. The Android app does not even declare the INTERNET permission.

What happens to my games and progress?

They stay on your device, in the app's private storage. On iPhone and iPad you can optionally sync them between your own devices through iCloud (off by default). On Android you can export a backup file and import it on another Android device.

Can I move my data between iPhone and Android?

No. The two apps do not exchange data: iCloud sync works between Apple devices, and the backup file is for Android devices only.

How do the human-like opponents work?

Nine characters are played by Maia-3, a neural network from the University of Toronto trained on millions of human games. It does not look for the best move: it plays the move a human of that level would play, mistakes included. Each character adds a style — attack, trades, gambits — without ever buying itself a blunder the model found unlikely.

What level are the opponents?

They use the human scale of Maia, close to the Lichess scale. It is not comparable with a Stockfish level: a Stockfish throttled to 1100 beats a Maia set at 2200, because it calculates where Maia recognises. Two scales, two different things. You can also play Stockfish itself, from about 900 to about 3190 Elo.

Which variants are included?

Chess960 and eleven others against the computer: King of the Hill, Three-Check, Horde, Atomic, Crazyhouse, Duck Chess, Barricades and more, each with its own analysis. There is also a two-player mode on one device.

Can I import my own games, repertoires and puzzles?

Yes. Load a game or a collection of games from a PGN file, paste a PGN or a FEN, build a position in the editor, or scan one from a photo. You can import your own opening repertoires as PGN, and your own puzzles as PGN files, which become collections you can drill, including with the Woodpecker method.

How does the scanner work?

Photograph a real board, or open a screenshot, and the position lands on the board. The recognition runs on your device and the image is sent nowhere. Whose turn it is, castling rights and en passant cannot be read from a picture, so the app does not guess: you set them.

How does the spaced repetition work?

Openings use FSRS-5, puzzles use SM-2. The unit is the position, not the line: a position you learned in one opening counts in every opening that reaches it. A daily session tells you what is due.

Which languages are supported?

French, English, Spanish, German and Italian, fully, including the opening and endgame courses and the coaching comments. The piece letters follow the language (K, Q, R… or R, D, T…), or you can choose them in the settings.

Why is it open source?

ChessLab embeds Stockfish, which is released under the GPLv3 licence. That licence requires the complete source code to be available, so it is, and so are the notices for the other components.

I found a bug, or I have an idea.

Write to thmaed@icloud.com or open an issue on GitHub. It is a one-person project with no guaranteed response time, but everything is read.
